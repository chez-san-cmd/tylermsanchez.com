Concert Radar

Presale alerts for Denver shows by artists I'd actually buy tickets for, early enough that popular shows don't push me to the resale market. Claude finds and flags; I buy every ticket myself.

What happened → what I changed

What happened. Leon Bridges at Red Rocks showed up in the Thursday digest as "presale now through Thursday 10pm." By the time I read it on Monday, general on-sale had started. The weekly venue email turned out to be the last presale window, not the first.

What I changed. Every digest line now carries an absolute date and time; "tonight" means nothing in an email I read three days later. And I've started adding artist-level feeds: a Bandsintown account is in and being tuned, Seated and the Tier 1 artists' own mailing lists are next. Until those are complete, the Thursday email is still the main Tier 2 source and I'll keep missing some early presales.

What it does

Two tiers. Tier 1 is the artists I know I like: everyone I've bought tickets for, saved on Spotify, or play the most. A match sends a push to my phone right away and, if the presale is still ahead, puts a fifteen-minute hold on my calendar at presale start. Tier 2 is discovery: artists Claude thinks I might like, or similar to what I already listen to, collected quietly into a Thursday-morning digest while that day's presales are still open. Each pick comes with one sentence on what the artist sounds like and a Spotify link, because my knowledge of newer artists has lapsed over the past decade.

Two scheduled runs a week, Tuesday and Thursday morning, each searching six days back through the venue and promoter newsletters already landing in my Gmail: AEG's Thursday "New Shows" list, Live Nation venues, the Paramount, the Botanic Gardens, Seated, Spotify's concert emails, and since mid-October Bandsintown. Matches become rows in a Notion table with the presale window, the type (artist, venue, registration, card), the password if there is one, the ticket link, and any calendar conflict on the show date. Tier 1 presales also get a tile on my Assistant Dashboard.

Why I built it

I already got the main feed. The gaps were filtering, timing, and coverage outside AEG. The real lesson was timing: Hans Zimmer at Ball Arena needed a registration a week before the presale, and the code arrived the day before. For the highest-demand shows, the alert that matters is the announcement-day one, not the presale-day one. By the time a show hits the Thursday list, an earlier artist or venue presale may already have run.

How it's built

  • Taste List. A Notion table of artists with a tier (Tier 1, Tier 2, or Skip), the evidence for the tier, and notes. 34 artists at launch, 23 in Tier 1, seeded from Spotify's most-played and saved lists, two years of ticket orders in Gmail, and Spotify's own concert recommendations. The runs read it fresh every time, and Tier 2 rows are Claude's guesses: I move one to Tier 1 or Skip to teach it.
  • Two Claude scheduled tasks that share one instruction set and re-read a "Run rules" section of the project's Notion book every run, so changing the behavior means editing a paragraph, not a prompt in code. The rules cover radius (Fort Collins to Colorado Springs plus the mountain venues for Tier 1, metro Denver for Tier 2), the eight-pick weekly cap on Tier 2, support acts counting as Tier 2, and a rule that every time is written as an absolute ("closes Thu Oct 15, 10pm"), never "tonight."
  • Connectors. Gmail for the newsletters (a filter archives them so they skip my inbox, and the Radar push becomes the signal), Spotify for taste and links, Google Calendar for conflict checks and holds, Notion for the record.
  • Boundaries. The runs are instructed never to buy, register, or join a queue, and never to write to the Attention Queue: this is the one fun project allowed to send phone alerts directly, because presale windows last hours.

The code is the two task prompts plus the Notion schemas. A standalone version isn't on the roadmap yet; if it lands, it'll be on GitHub.

What I learned

Registration is the real deadline. For the biggest shows the presale needs a registration that closes days earlier, and the code arrives the day before. So the alert that matters is the one on announcement day, which no venue roundup gives you. That reframed the project from "watch presales" to "watch announcements."

Twice a week is a real trade-off. Moving from daily to Tuesday and Thursday cut noise, but a presale announced late Thursday and closed by Tuesday is missed. I took that trade knowingly; most of what I'd buy sells slower than that.

A seed list is a guess. Spotify's connector returns a handful of artists per query, so the Taste List started partial, and the Tier 2 picks are Claude's inferences about what I'd like. Moving one to Tier 1 or Skip is the whole training interface, and it's enough.
